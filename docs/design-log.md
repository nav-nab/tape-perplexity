## Goal

- Laptop-scale ML research project in finance: train a small transformer on tokenised BTC trade sequences and test whether its perplexity ("model confusion") tracks volatility regimes. It is a regime detector, not a price predictor.
- The claim is detection (perplexity tracks regimes). A lead or early-warning test is left for later, only if the first result is strong.

## Data

- Binance spot BTCUSDT aggregated trades (data.binance.vision). Crypto was chosen because the non-crypto options cost money.
- Study period settled: 1 September to 30 November 2025 (UTC), timestamps in microseconds. Chronological split: September and October for training (61 days), November for testing (30 days). Reason: September is very calm, the training months include the 10 October crash, and November has several separate stressed stretches (14 of 30 test days above the training 90th percentile of a daily volatility proxy).
- Records with zero price or zero quantity are dropped.
- Training strictly before testing.

## Tokeniser

- No time fields in the tokens. Records are ordered by aggregate trade ID.
- Merge by default: consecutive records form one event (one taker order) when they have the same side and the same timestamp, and the price moves strictly in the aggressor's direction (higher for buys, lower for sells). The trade-ID contiguity test is dropped.
- Decision 1 settled: the price move of a merged event is its last fill price minus the previous event's last fill price, so it covers the order's whole impact.
- Decision 2 settled: the merge test is side, timestamp, and price strictly continuing in the aggressor's direction.
- Decision 3 settled: the price move is measured in ticks (0.01 USDT, whole numbers), not basis points.
- Decision 4 settled: magnitude bins per direction are: bin 1 is exactly 1 tick; bins 2 and 3 split the moves of 2 or more ticks at their training median. Edges are fit on absolute moves in the training period and shared between up and down moves.
- Decision 5, part 1 settled: size is measured in BTC (the native unit of the data), not USDT notional.
- Decision 5, part 2 settled: size bins are log-spaced, not quantile bins. Resolution is 3 bins per decade, with edges at $5\times10^{-5}\cdot10^{k/3}$ BTC. Tail rule: any top or bottom bin holding under about 1% of training events is merged into its neighbour, so the outermost bins are open-ended. Reason: log scale matches how humans perceive magnitude, fitting the "linguifying the tape" view of tokenisation.
- One composite token per event with three fields: side (2), size (log-spaced bins with tail merge, at most 16), price move (7: flat plus 3 magnitude bins per direction, i.e. $k=3$). Vocabulary at most $2\times16\times7=224$, single softmax.
- Only the tail-merge rule and the price-move magnitude edges are fit to the training period. The log size edges are fixed constants.

## Model, signal, evaluation

- Small causal transformer trained for next-token prediction.
- Model settled: medium, 6 layers, width 192, 6 heads, about 2.8M parameters, context 512 events. Three seeds (0, 1, 2) with one recipe. One model, chosen by the lowest final training loss (not by anything from the test month), is used for all surprisal results. The other seeds check that the losses are similar. Reason: the laptop is CPU-only (Intel i5-1038NG7, 4 cores); measured about 62 minutes per training pass, so about 3.1 hours for three seeds. Only the selected seed is scored (about 2 hours), and preparation takes 15 to 20 minutes, so the whole run is about 5 hours.
- Test-time scoring settled: sliding 512-event windows over the test stream with a stride of 64, scoring only the last 64 events of each window, so every scored event has 448 to 511 events of history (stride 1 would take about 5.5 days). The first November events take their history from the last 511 October events, which are context only. Estimated cost about 2 hours on the laptop CPU (25 million events, range 2 to 3 hours); only the selected model is scored.
- Training settled: one pass over the training stream, no separate validation set (each batch is new data), non-overlapping shuffled chunks of 512 events, AdamW with peak learning rate 1e-3, warm-up and cosine decay, weight decay 0.1, gradient clipping 1.0, about 16,000 tokens per step, no dropout, weights frozen for the test month.
- Primary signal: rolling mean surprisal over the last $N$ events (perplexity). Settled: $N=M=3000$, so both series summarise the same events with no extra tuned parameter.
- Volatility measure settled: root mean square of the per-event tick moves over the last $M$ events (plain realised volatility in event time). No jump-robust or noise-corrected variants. $M=3000$ events settled, chosen on 23 training days: one large sweep supplies 5 to 8% of a window's variance, a window spans about 12 minutes on a calm day, and adjacent windows agree with correlation about 0.6 (median 0.61) in log RV. Regime threshold settled: the 90th percentile of RV over all training events (about 251 ticks in a check on the 61 training days). The 10 October crash supplies 45% of flagged events (threshold would be about 222 ticks without it; it is kept). The flag is used for shading in plots and does not enter the headline correlation.
- Evaluation: plot perplexity against realised volatility over time, report the correlation on the test period, block-bootstrap confidence interval. Settled: moving blocks of 1,000,000 events (about one trading day, about 25 blocks in the test month), 2,000 replicates. Reason: on the training period the autocorrelation of log RV is still 0.15 to 0.20 at 1,000,000 events, and the bootstrap standard error keeps growing with block length (83 times the independence value at 1,000,000 events), so short blocks would be far too confident. The block-length check is repeated on the surprisal series of the test month by the evaluation stage (standard error against block length, plus autocorrelations).
- Left out by default: lead tests, GARCH or trade-intensity baselines, order book data, multiple architectures or token schemes.
- Token icons settled: every token has an icon in the "comet" style (colour = side, dot radius = size bin, tail = price move). The tail length is measured beyond the dot edge, so the same move has the same visible tail at every size. Specification in `docs/token-icons.md`. Used for the inference figure and a logo tape. Candle, heat tile and depth bar styles were prototyped and rejected.
- Generation GUI settled: the user gives a starting sequence of events and the model generates new tokens autoregressively, shown as icons. It does not compute surprisal and is outside the claim under test. Prompts come from a real test-month stretch (the committed excerpt file) or a clicked sequence, using test-month days only. Controls: number of events, temperature, number of continuations (default 1), seed. The real continuation is shown beneath the generated one. Icons only, no price line. Python process on the laptop, local server with a browser front end, separate module. See project design section 9.
- Code design settled: the whole study runs from one script that calls stage modules in order (download, clean and merge, fit the tokeniser and tokenise, train three seeds, select seed, score, evaluate, export the files kept in the repo). Checkpoint every 10 minutes of wall time with automatic resume on rerun. Heartbeat every minute with stage progress and a whole-run ETA. Extensive comments, well-organised modules. The GUI is a separate command, not a stage. See project design section 10.
- Final model weights are stored and committed (needed by the generation GUI): the selected seed's final weights only, about 11 MB, in `models/`, together with the fitted tokeniser and the model settings. Checkpoints, other seeds and data stay out of git. The script writes the files; the commit is made after the run.
- The data pipeline is part of the single script (its first stages). The export stage also writes a small prompt-excerpt file for the GUI: ten test-month stretches (511 prompt events plus the 256 that followed), drawn at random with a fixed seed, five from regime-flagged and five from unflagged stretches. It is committed with the weights so the GUI works from a clone.
- Implementation written and tested (2026-10-09): unit tests, synthetic end-to-end runs including a SIGTERM interrupt-and-resume equivalence check, and a five-day real-data smoke run. Choices made in the code, all recorded in the docs: data held as integer ticks and lots; first event of the stream dropped; moves equal to the training median count as "mid"; every download checked against Binance's SHA-256; any setting can be overridden with `--set`; changed settings refuse to resume (`--reset` clears saved state, keeps downloads); the bootstrap uses test events from the first one with both windows full; windows with zero volatility are clipped before the log. The block-length check on the surprisal series was first planned on the training period, which needs 2.5 extra hours of scoring. The user agreed to run it on the test month instead: it is the standard practice (choose the block length from the series being resampled), and it measures dependence only. The whole run is about 5 hours.
- Code audit against the design (2026-10-09): all settings match. Choices that the notes had not spelled out are now recorded in the design: AdamW betas (0.9, 0.95), 100 warm-up steps, weight decay on matrices only, last partial batch dropped. Two tests added (scoring resume equals uninterrupted; full-run settings match the design).
- Results analysed (2026-10-10): the full run finished. Headline correlation 0.56 (95% interval 0.42 to 0.60). A rarity benchmark (score each event by the rarity of its token in training) correlates 0.68 with log volatility, so part of the link is built in; the model stays about 1.1 nats below the benchmark in every volatility decile. Write-up settled as two parts: (1) the model is surprised by volatility; (2) but less surprised than rarity alone would suggest. The benchmark and other diagnostics were designed after seeing the result and are labelled as such. Paper source in `paper/` (TeX, bibliography, one script that makes every figure, number and table from the run outputs).
- Paper revised (2026-10-10): the paper is written for a reader who knows machine learning but not finance. Appendix A now explains the market terms (taker and maker orders, sweeping, ticks and lots, volatility) and Section 2.1 points to it. The abstract is shortened and gives no precision beyond the headline correlation. Detection versus prediction is discussed in the body only. Figure 1 puts volatility under mean surprisal with both feeding a coloured correlation box. The token comet icons appear as a key figure in Section 2.1. Figure 4b adds the transformer's own correlation next to the rarity bars. Lists inside paragraphs became bullets throughout. The previous-token model is a count table and not a neural network; its smoothing was chosen on held-out training data (alpha 0.5, last 15% of training) and the result did not change (3.51 nats, 73% of the transformer's gain). The Limitations section no longer describes the process of the study and drops the paragraph on analysis added after the result.
- Paper appendix on the inference interface added (2026-10-10): describes the generation GUI (sources, controls, what it reads) and shows a screenshot of the real interface after one generation. The example is the first stored excerpt (volatile-1), two continuations of 18 events at temperature 1, seed 1, chosen by rule and not for quality. The paper states that the example is an illustration and not evidence. The screenshot is made by `paper/make_gui_screenshot.py` against a running GUI.
